01 / PROBLEM STATEMENT

◀ FATIGUE INITIATION • SCALE: NTS ▶

A shaft carrying a load far below its yield strength still snaps after thousands of turns.

We simulate when, why and how to stop it.

Repeated cyclic stress, even below yield, starts a tiny crack at the surface or at a notch.

The crack grows a little every cycle with every single revolution of the shaft.

When the remaining area is too small, the part breaks suddenly with almost no warning.

TEAM OBSERVATION: Steel gets tired just like mechanical engineering students during submissions—except steel doesn't drink tea, it just snaps.

Steel has a safe zone. Aluminium never fully does.

Steels and titanium alloys show an endurance limit; most non-ferrous alloys such as aluminium do not.

AISI 1045 is our baseline because it is the most common carbon steel for industrial drive shafts.

Our Model Assumptions:
  • Fully reversed rotating bending load (R = -1, zero mean stress).
  • Ambient room temperature operating conditions without thermal creep.
  • Clean, dry environment without chemical corrosion or pitting.
  • Simply supported shaft with a concentrated central point load.

HOW TO READ A BROKEN SHAFT:
ORIGIN: Where the crack began (often a sharp keyway, notch or deep scratch).
BEACH MARKS: Rest lines left as the crack front advanced during cyclic load shifts.
FAST-FRACTURE: Sudden final snap. Small fast zone = low nominal stress; large fast zone = high stress overload.

1. BEST CHEAP FIX: Smoothing the notch from Kt 2.0 to a 1.5 fillet multiplies life by 6.4x at almost zero extra manufacturing cost.

2. BIGGEST OVERALL GAIN: A modest 20% increase in shaft diameter slashes nominal bending stress by 42%, shifting the shaft directly into infinite life.

3. RUNOUT ACHIEVED: Both a 20% diameter increase and upgrading to AISI 4140 alloy steel eliminate fatigue failure completely by achieving runout.

WORKSHOP REALITY: The cheapest way to make a shaft last forever is a 2-millimeter fillet radius; the most expensive way is a lawyer after it breaks.
DESIGN RULES WE LEARNED BEST PRACTICES
01. Avoid sharp corners and keyways with sharp edges; specify generous fillet radii at all steps.
02. Finish or polish critical surfaces to eliminate microscopic machining notch roots and tool tear marks.
03. Keep working stress strictly below the endurance limit with a minimum safety factor of 1.5.
04. Inspect for cracks periodically using NDT methods before micro-flaws reach critical crack size ac.

DYE PENETRANT (PT)
Capillary action draws bright colored liquid into surface-breaking cracks for direct visual inspection.
MAGNETIC PARTICLE (MT)
Magnetic leakage flux traps fine ferromagnetic particles along surface and near-surface cracks in steel.
ULTRASONIC TESTING (UT)
High-frequency acoustic echo reflections detect deep internal flaws, inclusions, and subsurface crack depth.
Student Lab Conclusion:

We tested and proved that fatigue breaks rotating shafts well below yield strength. Notches, surface roughness, and shaft diameter dictate operational life far more than alloy tensile strength alone. Simple engineering fixes—especially generous fillet radii and modest diameter increases—reliably turn rapid failures into infinite life.

FINAL AXIOM: In static loading, steel forgives; in cyclic fatigue loading, steel remembers every single turn.
